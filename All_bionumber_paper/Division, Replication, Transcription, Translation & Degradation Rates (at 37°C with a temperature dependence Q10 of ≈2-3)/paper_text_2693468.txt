Reduction of G1 phase duration and enhancement of c-myc gene expression in HeLa cells at hypergravity 

ABSTRACT
We have found that hypergravity stimulates the proliferation of HeLa cells through reduction of the Gt phase duration, concomitant with enhancement of c-myc gene expression. HeLa cells were grown in monolayer in culture flasks that were centrifuged to generate a constant 18, 35 or 70g at 37°C for up to 4 days. The cell proliferation was enhanced at 18, 35 and 70g, most notably at 35 g. Cell cycle analyses with [3H] thymidine (TdR)-colcemid treatment showed that the cell generation time in the 35 g culture was reduced by 17% as compared to the control, which was attributed to a 26% reduction of the Gi phase duration. No differences were observed in the duration of the S, G2 and M phases or in the [3H]TdR incorporation per S phase cell between the 35g culture and the control. The induction of c-myc gene expression was investigated by

RNA blot hybridization during a 15–360 min exposure of cells to 18, 35 and 70g. Elevated levels of c-myc mRNA were observed after a 15-min exposure, and maintained after a 360-min exposure at all hypergravities examined. The highest induction rate of c-myc mRNA was 3·8-fold higher than the control after a 120-min exposure to 35 g. The 35g condition was the most effective hypergravity for stimulating both cell proliferation and c-myc gene expression. Our study suggests that the appropriate level of hypergravity stimulates HeLa cell proliferation by reducing the G1 phase duration without affecting DNA synthesis rate, mediated through induction of c-myc gene expression.

Keywords:c-myc, proto-oncogenes, cell cycle, gravity, cell proliferation.

INTRODUCTION
Human embryonic lung WI-38 cells were cultured in space on Skylab 3 flight in 1973. No differences were observed in the growth curve, optical density of nuclei, migration, ultrastructure, mitotic index, karyotype or chromosome banding pattern of the cells between the flight and control culture (Montgomery et al. 1974). Recently, on Spacelab 1 flight (Cogoli et al. 1984) and Spacelab D-l flight (Bechler et al. 1986), it was shown that the [3H] thymidine (TdR) incorporation into trichloroacetic acid(TCA)-insoluble substances of cultured human lymphocytes, activated by concanavalin A in space, was reduced as compared to the ground control. The productivity of interferon in cultured human lymphocytes was enhanced aboard Soviet space laboratory Solyut (Tálas et al. 1984). Hypergravity generated by centrifugation enhanced the proliferation of cultured mammalian cells (Tschopp & Cogoli, 1983) and suppressed the morphogenesis in vitro of embryonic mouse limbs (Duke, 1983). These studies suggest that gravitational alteration may affect the proliferation and differentiation of mammalian cells in vitro. The proliferation of mammalian cells is believed to be regulated in the G] phase, the most variable in its duration of all the phases in the cell cycle (Prescott, 1976). A proto-oncogene c-myc is known to play an important role in cellular proliferation (Kelly & Siebenlist, 1986) and differentiation (Dmitrovsky et al. 1986). Thus the variation in the Gt phase duration and the induction of c-myc gene expression are of particular interest when considering the effects of hypergravity on mammalian cells.

Although many studies have been conducted to clarify the mechanism of gravity-sensation in plant cells (Halstead & Dutcher, 1987), no studies have been carried out on the cell cycle progression or the gene expression of mammalian cells in conditions of altered gravity. The purpose of this study is to clarify the way in which gravitational alteration affects the proliferation of HeLa cells from the point of cell cycle progression and protooncogenes induction.

MATERIALS AND METHODS
Hypergravity cell culture
HeLa cells were cultured in monolayer with Eagle’s minimum essential medium (Nissui Seiyaku, Tokyo, Japan) supplemented with 2mM-L-glutamine and 10% calf serum (Flow Laboratories, McLean, VA). The cells were dispersed in Dulbecco’s phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing 0·1% trypsin and 0·02% EDTA (Sigma Chemical, St Louis, MO). An equal number of cells were plated in 25 cm2 tissue culture flasks (LUX Miles Laboratories, Naperville, IL) and incubated until the cells were attached to the substratum. The culture flasks were filled with the medium and fastened tightly with screw caps, then placed in the swinging bucket rotor of a centrifuge (Kubota, Tokyo, Japan), which was kept in a room at 37°C. The cells in the culture flasks were centrifuged at 350, 500 or 700 revs min-1 to be exposed to a constant 18, 35 or 70 g force for up to 4 days. Culture flasks for controls were placed on the lid of the centrifuge and incubated for the same time with centrifuged cultures to obtain the identical conditions without centrifugation. The temperature inside and outside the centrifuge was monitored throughout the experiments to be kept precisely at 37·0°C. The cells were observed by phase-contrast microscopy immediately after termination of centrifugation. The decanted medium and washes were collected to eliminate the effects of centrifugation on cell yields. The temperature and pH of the decanted medium were measured immediately to ascertain that experiments were conducted under identical conditions. The number of cells in each flask was counted with a hemocytometer to estimate the proliferation of cells.

[3H] thymidine incorporation
During the last 2h on the second, third and fourth days of each hypergravity culture, the cells were labeled with 0·1 μCi ml-1 of [3H]TdR (20 Ci mmol-1, New England Nuclear, Boston, MA). The cells were rinsed with PBS, then harvested in PBS containing trypsin and EDTA, and rinsed three times in cold 5% TCA solution. The TCA-insoluble precipitates were hydrolyzed at 90°C (Erwin et al. 1981). The radioactivity in the hydrolysate was measured by a scintillation counter (Packard 460, Downers Grove, IL). In experiments determining the labeling index, the cells were cultured at 35 g for 3 days and labeled for the last 20 min with the culture medium containing l·5pCiml-1 of [3H]TdR, then fixed with 2% glutaraldehyde solution. The percentage of cells incorporating [3H]TdR was estimated by autoradiography. The labeling index was obtained from 6000 cells for each culture.

Cell cycle analysis
On the fourth day of the 35 g culture, cells were labeled for 20min with medium containing l·5pCiml-1 of [3H]TdR and 0·04μgml-1 of colcemid, then rinsed and subsequently incubated at 35 g with medium containing 0·04 μg ml-1 of colcemid for 5 h, as described by Watanabe & Okada (1967). The decanted medium and washes were collected for precise analyses. The percentage of cells incorporating [3H]TdR was estimated by autoradiography. The durations of the G1, S, G2 and M phases were obtained from 6000 cells for each culture.

RNA blot hybridization (Northern analysis)
The logarithmically proliferating cells were exposed to 18, 35 or 70g, each for 15, 30, 60, 120 or 360min. Immediately after termination of centrifugation, total RNA was extracted from the cells in 4M-guanidinium isothiocyanate and 0·5% sodium lauryl sarcosinate solution (Chirgwin et al. 1979), then reextracted with phenol/chloroform (1/1, v/v) at 60°C (Fer-amisco et al. 1982) and precipitated in ethanol at —30°C. The concentration of the RNA was determined by reading the absorbance at 260 nm, while the purity of the RNA was checked by measuring the ratio of absorbance at 260 and 280 nm (Chomczynski & Sacchi, 1987). A 10 or 15 μg sample of RNA was loaded in each lane of 1-5% agarose gel (FMC Seakem, Rockland, ME) containing 7% formaldehyde, 20mM-morpho-linopropanesulfonic acid (pH 7·0), 5 mM-sodium acetate and 1mM-EDTA, and subjected to electrophoresis, then transferred onto nylon membranes (Hybond-N, Amersham, Arlington Heights, IL) for 24 h with 20X standard saline-citrate (SSC is 0·15M-NaCl, 0·015 M-sodium citrate, pH 7·0) (Meinkoth & Wahl, 1984). The gel was stained with Acridine Orange to visualize the 28 S and 18 S ribosomal RNAs, confirming that equal amounts of RNA were subjected to gel electrophoresis. The RNA was crosslinked to the nylon membranes by ultraviolet light irradiation. The membranes were hybridized at 42°C for 36 h in 50% deionized formamide solution (Nakarai, Kyoto, Japan) containing 0·9M-NaCl, 50 mM-sodium phosphate (pH7·7), 5mM-EDTA, 5 × Denhardt’s solution, 0·1% sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS), 0·lmgml-1 of denatured salmon testes DNA (Sigma) and heat-denatured 32P-labeled probes. The Clal-EcoRÏ fragments of exon 3 of human cellular myc gene, supplied by Dr Kiyoshi Nose of Institute of Medical Science, University of Tokyo, were labeled with [o,-32P]dCTP (3000 Ci mmol-1, New England Nuclear, Boston, MA) by nicktranslation (approximately 1× 105 disintsmin-1 pg-1). Following hybridization the membranes were rinsed three times in 1× SSC and 0·1% SDS at 25 °C then washed three times in 0·2 × SSC and 0·1% SDS at 42°C. Hybrids were detected by autoradiography at —80°C for 5 or 10 days using X-ray film (Kodak X-Omat AR).

The chemicals and reagents, unless manufacturers were mentioned, were purchased from Wako Pure Chemicals, Osaka, Japan.

Data analysis
The statistical significance of data was evaluated by Student’s t-test.

RESULTS
The growth curves for the 35 g and control cultures were obtained as linear lines, in which logarithmic regression analyses determined the correlation coefficient, 0·99 for both cultures (Fig. 1). The HeLa cells proliferated logarithmically from the second through the fourth day in the 35 g and control cultures. During the first 2 days, there was no difference in the number of cells between the 35 g culture and the control. However, the number of the cells in the 35 g culture increased to 138 ± 4% of the control on the third day, and 152 ± 12% on the fourth day. The population doubling time was 13·7 h in the 35 g culture and 16·5 h in the control.

We focused on the induction of a proto-oncogene c-myc in HeLa cells in response to hypergravity. The induction of c-myc gene expression was observed in the cells exposed to a constant 18, 35 or 70g for 15 through 360min (Fig. 4). It was shown by densitometry that the abundance of c-myc mRNA was significantly increased after a 15–360 min continuous exposure to any of 18, 35 or 70 g, as compared to the controls, which were not exposed to hypergravity (Table 3). The 35g treatment resulted in elevating the levels of c-myc mRNA to 3·0fold and 3·8-fold higher than the control after 30 and 120 min, respectively. The elevated level of c-myc mRNA was maintained at 3·3-fold higher than the control even after a 360-min exposure to 35 g. Similar results were obtained in the cells exposed to 18 or 70 g. The level of c-myc mRNA was elevated higher than the control after a 15-mm exposure to 18 or 70 g, and 2-to 3-fold higher than the control after a 30-360 min exposure to 18 or 70 g. After a 30-min exposure to hypergravity, the relative abundance of c-myc mRNA was 1·8-fold, 3·0fold and 2·4-fold higher than each control in the 18, 35 and 70 g treatment, respectively. Similar results were obtained after a 120-min exposure to hypergravity: The level of c-myc mRNA was 2·5-fold, 3·8-fold and 2-6-fold higher than each control in the 18, 35 and 70 g treatment, respectively. The 35 g treatment induced the highest relative abundance of c-myc mRNA as compared to the control after a 30- and 120-min exposure to hypergravity. These data indicated that the 35 g treatment was the most effective for inducing c-myc gene expression in HeLa cells.

DISCUSSION
Particular attention was taken in our study to exclude the possibility of false-positive results. Hypergravity was generated by centrifugation in this study (Smith et al. 1979). The Coriolis force was negligible, since it was calculated to be less than 5× 10∽6 of the centrifugal force. The effects of the hydrostatic pressure variation (Tschopp & Cogoli, 1983), vibration and the gradient accumulation or depletion of nutrients and cellular waste products were almost eliminated by conducting control experiments, in which HeLa cells were cultured on the substratum of Leighton-type tubes and exposed to hypergravity force in all four directions (Kumei et al. unpublished). Since temperature can regulate cell proliferation, we have measured the temperature of the medium in culture flasks, 37·0 ± 0·1 deg. C for the hypergravity cultures and controls at the beginning and end of experiment. The temperature inside and outside the centrifuge was maintained at 37·0 ± 0·1 deg. C throughout the experiment. The pH was 7·39 ± 0·01 for the culture medium at the beginning, and 7·39 ±0·02 or 7·38 ±0·02 for the decanted medium at the end of experiments, in each culture. Hence the temperature or pH should not have any causative role in reduction of the G1 phase duration or elevation of c-myc gene expression. Since the half-life time of c-myc mRNA of HeLa cells was reported to be extremely short (Dani et al. 1984), we finished the RNA extraction exactly 5 min after termination of hypergravity exposure. Although other factors such as the hydrodynamic shearing stress, the convection, the fluctuation of dissolved O2 and the moment of intracellular macromolecules should be taken into consideration for future studies, we conclude that the reduction in the Gi phase duration and the enhanced expression of c-myc gene are reflections of HeLa cell response to hypergravity.

Hypergravity at 18, 35 and 70g’ enhanced HeLa cell proliferation, which was attributed to reduction in the Gi phase duration in the cell cycle. The total [3H]TdR incorporation was increased most prominently in the 35 g culture. The expression of c-myc gene was elevated promptly in response to hypergravity at 18, 35 and 70g’. The 35 g’ environment was the most effective hypergravity for enhancing c-myc gene expression, consistent with the results of the total [3H]TdR incorporation.

It has been reported that the level of c-myc mRNA is increased by growth factors that induce ‘competence’ in cultured quiescent cells (Kelly et al. 1983). This activation has been preceded by a rapid and transient increase in c-fos gene expression (Millier et al. 1984) and followed by c-myb induction (Thompson et al. 1986). We have not detected the enhanced expression of c-fos or c-myb gene in response to hypergravity (Kumei et al. unpublished). The hypergravity mode of action in HeLa cell proliferation may not be identical to the action of the growth factors. However, the v-fos gene has been reported to function in transactivation of transcription, suggesting that fos is a transcription regulator (Setoyama et al. 1986). The levels of c-fos mRNA have been reported to be extremely low or even undetectable throughout the cell cycle but to be slightly elevated in growing cells compared to quiescent cells (Müller & Bravo, 1986). Hence, we cannot deny the possibility that we have failed to detect slight changes in the c-fos gene expression in response to hypergravity.

The levels of c-myc mRNA have been found to be regulated both transcriptionally and post-transcription-ally (Marcu, 1987) and to be invariant throughout the cell cycle (Thompson et al. 1985). The role of hypergravity may be involved in the hypothesis that deregulated expression of c-myc gene modulates the expression of other proliferation-related genes (Cleveland et al. 1987). Alternatively, c-myc gene may encode a protein that is functionally involved in DNA replication (Studzinski et al. 1986). The G1 phase of HeLa cells has been considered to have some specific functions, since it cannot be reduced beyond certain limits (Rao et al. 1984). It has been proposed that c-myc gene products are critical for S-phase entry and/or S-phase traversal (Heikkila et al. 1987). Recently, it has been shown that the 3T3 cells, deprived of platelet-derived growth factor and fibroblast growth factor, can enter the S phase after induction of c-myc gene expression by glucocorticoid treatment (Cavalieri & Goldfarb, 1987). Thus the rate of cell cycle progression in the transition from the G1 to S phase, or Gi phase traversal, may be accelerated at hypergravity through stimulated expression of c-myc gene. Our results are consistent with the hypothesis that hypergravity induces enhanced expression of c-myc gene in HeLa cells with reduction in the G1 phase duration in the cell cycle.

The induction of c-myc gene expression may be attributed to chemical and physical alterations in the cytoskeleton as a result of hypergravity. Reports indicate that the cytoskeleton-associated 350 000Mr protein is rapidly phosphorylated after growth stimulation (Sato et al. 1985). This protein is transferred to the U1-ribo-nucleoprotein regions of the nucleus concomitant with elevated synthesis of c-myc mRNA (Sato et al. 1986). Hypergravity may phosphorylate the cytoskeleton-associated protein resulting in transcriptional activation of proliferation-related genes. Alternatively, extracellular signals that stimulate cell proliferation are mediated through phosphoinositide turnover, Ca2+ mobilization and protein kinase C activation (Berridge, 1987), in which the hypergravity action may be involved. Hypergravity may otherwise activate another protein kinase C-independent pathway that is implicated in the c-myc not c-fos induction (Hall & Stiles, 1987). However, the mechanism for transducing from the hypergravity sensation into a signal that can stimulate cell proliferation still remains to be investigated.
