Correlation between cell enlargement and nucleic acid and protein content of hela cells in unbalanced growth produced by inhibitors of DNA synthesis†

Abstract
HeLa cells in monolayer cultures were treated with the following inhibitors of DNA synthesis: mitomycin C, nitrogen mustard, fluorodeoxyuridine, hydroxyurea, arabinofuranosylcytosine and high concentrations of thymidine. The concentration of each inhibitor used was, in most cases, just sufficient to arrest cell multiplication and all produced unbalanced growth in the sense that the synthesis of RNA and protein were only partially inhibited while DNA synthesis stopped. This resulted in approximately 100% increases in RNA and protein content per cell in 48 hours and, since cell volume also increased by 100% during this time, the concentration of RNA and protein per unit cell volume remained constant. It was concluded that cell protein content may be used as an accurate index of variation in cell size in HeLa cells treated with inhibitors of DNA synthesis.

The synthesis of RNA and protein inmammalian cells can be dissociated fromthe synthesis of DNA by the use of specificinhibitors? For example, a number of anti-metabolites of therapeutic importance inthe palliative treatment of cancer and thenormal deoxynucleosides, when adminis-tered in amounts greatly exceeding thephysiological concentrations, inhibit thesynthesis of DNA and cell division butallow the synthesis of RNA and protein tocontinue for a time. The condition of thetreated cells has been called "unbalancedgrowth (Eidinoff and Rich, '59; Rueckertand Mueller, '60) in analogy to the similarbut better characterized situation in micro-organisms (Cohen and Barner, '54, '56).Unbalanced growth has been shown totake place in cultured mammalian cellstreated with amethopterin (Rueckert andMueller, '60), FUD R k (Eidinoff and Rich,'59), HN2 (Brewer et al., '61; Layde andBaserga, '64), MC (Kuroda and Furuyama,'65), excess Td R (Kim et al., '65), and twodrugs of recent interest in cancer chemo-therapy, AraC (Karon et al., '66; Kimand Eidinoff, '65) and HU (Young andHodas, '64).

Several groups of investigators have al-ready noted that the accumulation of RNAand protein in unbalanced growth of mammalian cells is accompanied by cell en-largement (Brewer et d., '61; Karon et d.,'66; Klein and Forssberg, '54; Kuroda andFuruyama, '65; Lapis and Bernhard, '65;Layde and Baserga, '64; Levis et al., '65;Shatkin et al., '62). However, with thenotable exception of the experiments ofKlein and Forssberg ('54), this observationwas based on the larger appearance ofstained cells examined microscopically; itwas not quantitatively evaluated, and thecell enlargement was not correlated withthe increase in the cell RNA and proteincontent.

The aim of the experiments presentedhere was to compare and contrast theeffects on HeLa cells of a number of in-hibitors of DNA synthesis with differingmodes of action (fig. l ) , paying particularattention to the correlation of the cell RNAand protein content with the cell volume.It was found that treatment with cytostaticconcentrations of the inhibitors of DNAsynthesis led to approximately equivalent increases in cell RNA, cell protein and cellsize. Since these changes did not dependon tbe mode of action of the agent used totreat the cells, they were considered to rep-resent some of the basic characteristics of"unbalanced growth."
These findings have already been brieflyreported (Studzinski and Cohen, '66b).

MATERIALS AND METHODS
Tissue cultureCulture conditions have been describedin previous reports from this laboratory(Studzinski and Ellem, '66; Studzinski andLove, '64), but the cells in this study wereHeLa (-af), a strain cloned by Saksela etal. ('61). The cells were propagated asmonolayers in plastic culture bottles andgrown in Eagle's minimal essential me-dium supplemented with 8% inactivatedhorse serum, 1% glutamine, and either0.02% kanamycin or 0.005% aureomycin.The cells required for each experimentwere suspended in sterile versene, dilutedwith the appropriate amount of medium, and seeded in 10 ml of medium on glassPetri dishes (60 mm diameter) using aCornwall pipet. Three days were allowedfor the cells to adhere to the Petri dishesand to enter the logarithmic phase ofgrowth. As indicated in the text below, itwas found useful at times to grow the cellsin "ring cultures" prepared by sealingstainless steel rings onto circular cover-slips (22 mm diameter) with a parafEn-vaseline mixture, as described by Love andWildy ('63). The monolayers were incu-bated at 37°C in a humidified atmosphereof 5% COP in air. When logarithmicgrowth was established, the medium waswithdrawn from some cultures and re-placed with 10 ml of medium containingdissolved inhibitor. Other cultures wererefed with normal medium and served ascontrols. The cells were tested and foundto be free of PPLO contamination by Dr.L. Hayflick of The Wistar Institute.

Cell counting and cell sizeCell number was obtained with a Coul-ter counter, while cell sizing was performed with the automatic particle sizedistribution analyser Model J (CoulterElectronic Co., Hialeah, Fla.). Cell vol-ume was also measured by centrifuging aknown number of cells at 850 g to constantvolume in a hematocrit tube.

Staining methodIn each experiment, coverslips werepresent in some of the Petri dishes and, atthe conclusion of the experiment, werestained and examined microscopically inparallel with biochemical analyses andsize determinations on the remainder ofthe cells. The coverslips were rinsed inisotonic saline, fixed for ten minutes inLillie's acetic - alcohol - formalin mixture(Lillie, '49), and stained with 0.5% to-luidine blue in citrate buffer at pH 3.0.Quantitation of the cell enlargement wasattempted by measurement of two cell di-ameters at right angles to each other,using a calibrated scale in the eyepiece ofthe microscope.

Biochemical proceduresCells were harvested from Petri dishes,after removal of the culture medium, bybrief incubation in versene solution andtransferred with a syringe into graduatecylinders in an ice bath. The cells wereenumerated and distributed into triplicatealiquots of 1.5 X 10' cells for separationof the nucleic acids by a modified Schmidt-Thannhauser procedure (Fleck and Munro,'62). DNA content was measured by Bur-ton's ('56) diphenylamine reaction at 37°Cusing thymus DNA heated in 0.6 N per-chloric acid for 20 minutes at 90°C as thestandard. RNA was determined by the opti-cal density at 260 mu of the alkali digest.The residue of the Schmidt-Thannhauserextraction was dissolved in 1 N NaOH andits protein content determined by themethod of Lowry et al. ('51) using crystal-line egg albumin (N.B. Co.) that had beencarried through the Schmidt-Thannhauserprocedure, as standard.

InhibitorsMC and HU were obtained from N.B. Co.(Cleveland, Ohio), HN2 was purchasedfrom K and L Laboratories Inc. (Plainview,N.Y.) and TdR from Calbiochem (LosAngeles). AraC was kindly provided byDr. P. W. OConnell of the Upjohn Co.(Michigan), and FUDR by Hoffmann-La Roche (Nutley, NJ.).

Measurements of cell volume with Coul-ter Counter showed that there was no ap-preciable change in the volume of controlcells during the 48 hours of inhibitor treat-ment.

DISCUSSION
The principal feature of unbalancedgrowth noted in the present study, namely,doubling of the cell volume after 48 hours of treatment, with constancy of the ratiosof RNA and protein per unit cell volumeduring the enlargement, may be consideredto be an extension of the work of Klein andForssberg ('54) who found that this a pplies to irradiated Ehrlich ascites cells atvarious times, including 48 hours, afterinhibition of DNA synthesis has beenestablished. Similarly, Tolmach and Mar-cus ('60) found that nitrogen content ofx-ray treated HeLa cells was directly proportional to the increased cell volume.These observations considered together in-dicate that the constancy of the ratio ofRNA:protein:cell volume may be a basiccharacteristic of unbalanced growth pro-duced by selective inhibition of DNA syn-thesis by any means, physical as well aschemical, and in cells other than HeLacells. Studies by Kraemer ('66) on HeLacells inhibited with TdR allow further ex-tension of the generality of the phenomenastudied here; Kraemer found that the sialicacid content of treated cells increases inparallel with the cell enlargement, and theincreases were of similar magnitude tothose found in the present study. It wouldappear, therefore, that, when cell divisionis prevented by inhibition of DNA synthe-sis or cross-linking of DNA, other cell con-stituents continue to be formed and thecell content of macromolecules can reachat least twice its average control value. Thesynthetic processes taking place duringthis unbalanced growth must be in largemeasure normal, since treated cells appearstructurally intact and adhere well to thesurfaces on which they grow. This is fur-ther supported by the observation that theactivity of cellular deoxyribonucleases pro-gressively increases in HeLa cell treatedwith the inhibitors of DNA synthesis, butnot in cells treated simultaneously with aninhibitor of DNA synthesis and puromycin(Studzinski and Cohen, '66a; Studzinskiet al., '66), which suggests that the in-creased enzyme activities in HeLa cellstreated with inhibitors of DNA synthesisresult from continued formation of en-zymically active proteins.

The constancy of the concentration perunit cell volume of both RNA and protein found here after treatment with inhibitorsof DNA synthesis implies that swelling byincreased uptake of water plays no part inthe cell enlargement characteristic of un-balanced growth. When HeLa cells weretreated with puromycin, an inhibitor ofprotein synthesis, they became smaller,and it can be calculated from data pre-sented by Studzinski and Love ('64) that,under those conditions, the RNA and pro-tein content per unit cell volume alsoremained constant. However, preliminaryunpublished experiments show that, aftertreatment with actinomycin D, RNA con-tent per unit HeLa cell volume decreasesmarkedly, while protein concentration re-mains constant. It can be concluded thatprotein content of HeLa cells treated withantimetabolites gives an accurate indica-tion of their size.
