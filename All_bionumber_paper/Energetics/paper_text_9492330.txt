Changes in the proton potential and the cellular energetics of Escherichia coli during growth by aerobic and anaerobic respiration or by fermentation

Abstract
The energetic parameters of Escherichia coli were analyzed for the aerobic/anaerobic transition. The electrochemical proton potential (Δ p) across the cytoplasmic membrane was determined in the steady state of respiration with O2, nitrate, fumarate, dimethylsulfoxide (Me2SO), and for fermentation. With O2, a proton potential of −160 mV was obtained. For anaerobic respiration with nitrate, fumarate or Me2SO, Δ p decreased only slightly by about 20 mV in contrast to earlier assumptions, whereas Δ p dropped by approximately 40 mV during fermentation. Under all conditions, the membrane potential (Δ @KY) contributed the major portion to Δ p. The cellular ATP levels were highest for aerobic growth (about 13 μmol/g dry cells) and decreased to 3−6 μmol/g in anaerobic metabolism. Δ .cf2.G.cf2..cf1..es.cf1.′.cf5..rb.eiPhos.rb, however, was constant due to equivalent changes of the ADP contents. Transition to the stationary growth phase caused a massive drop in the ATP content. It is concluded that, during anaerobic respiration, the energetic situation for the bacteria is very similar to that for aerobic growth with respect to Δ .cf2.G.cf2..cf1..es.cf1.′.cf5..rb.eiPhos.rb and Δ p whereas, for fermentation, a significant decrease in Δ p was observed. The consequences for the cellular energetics and for the regulation of the aerobic/anaerobic transition are discussed.

Keywords:  proton potential; membrane potential; anaerobic respiration, aerobic/anaerobic transition; Escherichia coli . Facultative anaerobes such as  Escherichia coli  are capable of growth both under oxic and anoxic conditions. The energetics and the ATP yields vary considerably for both conditions (Unden and Bongaerts,  1 997). The free energy for glucose oxidation with   $\mathrm{O}_{2}$     $(\mathcal{A}\,G_{0}^{\prime}=-2870~\mathrm{kJ/mol}$   glucose) exceeds that for glucose fermentation   $(\varDelta G_{0}^{\prime}=-218\;\mathrm{kJ/mol}$   glucose). Similarly, respiration with NADH yields much higher free energy with   $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   $(\varDelta G_{0}^{\prime}=-233\;\mathrm{kJ/mol}$   NADH) than with fumarate (  $\mathcal{A}G_{0}^{\prime}=$   $-67\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$   NADH). Therefore, it has been claimed that the proton potential   $(\varDelta p)$   and the phosphor yl ation potential   $(\varDelta G_{\mathrm{Phos}}^{\prime})$   of  $E.$  . coli  are decreased significantly under anoxic conditions. Although the respective energetic parameters have been analyzed repeatedly (Cole et al.,  1 967; Hell ing we rf et al.,  1 98 1 ; Kashket et al.,  1 98 1 ,  1 982,  1 983) this question has not been solved. In particular, the data are not complete and consider only the situation for aerobic respiration and fermentation, but not for anaerobic respiration. In addition the parameters of the various studies were not performed in a systematic manner which allowed direct comparison.  

This obvious lack in our knowledge on the energetic parameters of facultative anaerobic metabolism is contrasted by the significance of such parameters for the aerobic/anaerobic transition. There are indications that the proton potential has to be lowered in anaerobic respiration to enable the functioning of coupling enzymes such as NADH dehydrogenase I ( nuo  genes) as a proton pump (Bongaerts et al.,  1 995; Tran et al.,  1 997; Unden and Bongaerts,  1 997). In fumarate or dimethyl sulfoxide   $(\mathbf{MeV}_{2}\mathbf{SO})$  respiration, the enzyme has to use men aquino ne or demethylmen aquino ne as an electron acceptor (Tran et al.,  1 997). The electro-negative midpoint potential of men aquino ne   $\begin{array}{r l}{\relax(E^{\circ\prime}=}\end{array}$   $-80\:\mathrm{mV})$   results in a low redox difference between NADH

  $(E^{\circ\prime}=-320~\mathrm{mV})$  ) and men aquino ne. Under these conditions, NADH dehydrogenase I would be able to operate as a proton pump with the suggested ratio of   $2\mathrm{~\H^{+}/e^{-}}$    only at decreased membrane potentials. Moreover, it has been suggested that the presumed drop in  $\varDelta p$   between aerobic and anaerobic respiratory conditions could supply the regulatory signal for the   $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   sensor protein ArcB (Bogachev et al.,  1 993,  1 995) which is one of the major transcriptional regulators of aerobic and anaerobic metabolism (Iuchi and Lin,  1 993). In light of these deficites in our knowledge on the energetic status of    $E_{\uparrow}$  . coli  during aerobic and anaerobic growth, the membrane   $(\varDelta\varPsi)$  , proton   $(\varDelta p)$   and phosp hory la tion   $(\varDelta G_{\mathrm{Phos}}^{\prime})$   potentials were determined under conditions of aerobic and anaerobic respiration and of fermentation, in a manner which enabled direct comparison. It was shown that, in contrast to earlier assumptions, there are only minor changes in  $\varDelta\varPsi$  ,  $\varDelta p$   and  $\it{A G_{\mathrm{Phos}}^{\prime}}$   after switching from aerobic to anaerobic respiration. A more significant decrease in  $\varDelta\varPsi$   and  $\varDelta p$   was only observed for fermentation.

Measurement of the membrane potential   $(\varDelta\varPsi)$  .  Important energetic parameters concerning the energetic status of  $E$  . coli are represented by the membrane potential   $(\varDelta\varPsi)$  , the    $\varDelta\,\mathrm{pH}$   and the resulting electrochemical proton potential   $(\varDelta p)$  . These parameters were determined in    $E$  . coli  grown with glycerol plus the acceptors  $\mathrm{O}_{2}$  , nitrate,  $\mathrm{Me}_{2}\mathrm{SO}$   or fumarate, or by ferment at ive growth with glucose (Table 2). The membrane potential was determined from the distribution of   $\mathrm{{Pb}_{4}{P}^{+}}$    between the bacterial cells   $(\mathrm{CH_{4}P_{c e l l s}^{+}})$  ) and the medium   $\mathrm{(Ph_{4}P_{e x}^{+})}$  ) after reaching a steady state of electron transport and e qui lib ration of  $\mathrm{{Pb}_{4}{P}^{+}}$  . From   $\mathrm{{\bfDy}_{4}}$   $\mathsf{P}_{\mathrm{cells}}^{+}$    and the cytoplasmic volume   $(V_{\mathrm{i}})$  , the intracellular concentrations were calculated. The internal volumes were measured for the different growth conditions separately, since    $E$  . coli changes cell size and volume depending on the growth conditions and growth rate. The    $V_{\mathrm{in}}$   values indeed varied by a factor of 2 for the different growth conditions (Table 2).

Direct comparison of  $\pmb{A}\pmb{\Psi}$   from aerobic and anaerobic respiration in the same EDTA cells.  For a direct comparison of the effects of electron acceptors on    $\varDelta\varPsi$  , the bacteria were grown under conditions which caused the expression of alternative respiratory systems at the same time, then incubated with the alternative acceptors  $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   or fumarate (Table 3). In the first experiment, the bacteria were grown with glycerol plus fumarate under anoxic conditions. Under these conditions, the bacteria contained the fumarate respiratory system, but in addition the quinoloxidase    $_{b d}$   is produced at substantial levels (Fu et al.,  1 99 1 ; Tseng et al.,  1 996). In the EDTA-treated bacteria incubated with fumarate or   $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   as the acceptors, the distribution of   $\mathrm{{Pb}_{4}{P}^{+}}$    was determined. After correction for the bound   $\mathrm{{Pb}_{4}{P}^{+}}$  , very similar  $\varDelta\varPsi$   values were obtained for both acceptors (Table 3). In a second approach, the bacteria were grown with fumarate plus  $\mathrm{O}_{2}$  . Under these conditions, fumarate reductase is synthesized at high levels, despite the presence of  $\mathrm{O}_{2}$  . Thus, the EDTA-treated cells were capable of aerobic and of fumarate respiration (Becker and Unden, unpublished results). With  $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   as the acceptor, slightly higher  $\varDelta\varPsi$   values were obtained than for fumarate. # DISCUSSION  

ATP contents and  $\mathbf{\it{4G}_{\mathbf{oplus}}^{\prime}}$   as energetic parameters for faculta- tive anaerobic metabolism.  The ATP contents and  $\it{4G_{\mathrm{phys}}^{\prime}}$   were compared for    $E$  . coli  growing with various electron acceptors under oxic and anoxic conditions. The electron acceptors were the only varied parameters, to enable direct comparison. The values were at the upper level of previously published values (Mathis and Brown,  1 976; Holms et al.,  1 972; Cole et al.,  1 967; Lowry et al.,  1 97 1 ). The ATP contents not only decreased for fermentation (factor 2.4), but also for anaerobic respiration, in particular for  $\mathrm{Ma}_{2}\mathrm{SO}$   (factor 4.5) and fumarate (factor 2.6) respirations. The contents are not related to the aerobic/anaerobic transition, but there is a loose relation to the growth rates (Table 4) which had already been observed earlier (Bagnara and Finch, 1 973). The  $\varDelta G_{\mathrm{Phos}}^{\prime}$   values, however, were remarkably constant for the various conditions due to the corresponding changes in the ADP contents. Therefore,    $\it{\Delta}M G_{\mathrm{Phos}}^{\prime}$   cannot be used as a characteristic signal for the aerobic/anaerobic transition.  

High membrane   $(M\,\Psi)$   and proton   $(\pmb{M}p)$   potentials in anaerobic respiration.  The procedure of Zaritzky et al. ( 1 98 1 ) has been shown as a reliable procedure for measuring  $\varDelta\varPsi$   (Wloczyk et al.,  1 989; Mell et al.,  1 986). Here, for aerobic respiration,  $\varDelta\varPsi$   $(-140\:\mathrm{mV})$   and    $\varDelta p$   (  $.-160\;\mathrm{mV})$   values similar to earlier published data (Kashket et al.,  1 98 1 ;  1 982) were obtained. Most remarkably, the    $\varDelta p$   values (about    $-140\:\mathrm{mV})$  ) for anaerobic respiration with nitrate, fumarate and  $\mathrm{Mo}_{2}\mathrm{SO}$   decreased only up to  $20\:\mathrm{mV}$   compared to aerobic respiration. The results are in contrast to earlier experiments suggesting a decrease of    $\varDelta p$   for fumarate respiration to about    $-100\:\mathrm{mV}$   (Hell ing we rf et al.,  1 98 1 ). The presence of high    $\varDelta\varPsi$   and  $\varDelta p$   values in anaerobic respiration was also shown directly by measuring similar values when the same preparation of bacteria was incubated alternatively with  $\mathrm{O}_{2}$  or fumarate (Table 3). Thus, the situation for    $E$  . coli  appears to be similar to that for  Wolinella succ in o genes  which is known to build up high    $\varDelta p$   values   $(-170\:\mathrm{mV})$   with electro positive and electronegative acceptors (Wloczyk et al.,  1 989; Mell et al., 1 986). Possibly, in previous experiments, the presence of such  $\mathrm{{Pb}_{4}{P}^{+}}$    efflux systems prevented the generation of high    $\varDelta\varPsi$   values.  

High  $\varDelta p$   values during aerobic and anaerobic respiration are essential for the function of ATP synthase. From the  $\varDelta p$   and the  $\varDelta G_{\mathrm{Phos}}^{\prime}$   values, it can be calculated that  $3.1\!-\!3.4~\!\mathrm{H}^{+}$    are required for ADP phosphor yl ation if    $\varDelta p$   and    $\it{\Delta}M G_{\mathrm{Phos}}^{\prime}$   are in thermodynamic equilibrium (Table 4). This is in agreement with suggested  $\mathrm{H^{+}/A T P}$   ratios of  $_{3-4}$  . A supposed decrease of    $\varDelta p$   to  $-100\:\mathrm{mV}$   would require  $\mathrm{H^{+}/A T P}$   ratios close to 5, which is very unlikely. Therefore, the    $\varDelta p$   of anaerobic respiration indeed has to be as high as determined here. Only in fermentation, when ADP is phosphor y late d by substrate-level phosphor yl ation, is a decrease in    $\varDelta p$   to lower levels tolerated.  

Significance of high    $_{4p}$   values for the function of anaerobic respiration.  In    $E$  . coli , fumarate and   $\mathrm{Mo}_{2}\mathrm{SO}$   respiration with NADH depends on the proton pumping NADH dehydrogenase I (Bongaerts et al.,  1 995; Tran et al.,  1 997) which is supposed to trans locate   $2~\mathrm{H^{+}/e^{-}}$    (Friedrich et al.,  1 995; Bogachev et al., 1 996). For operation in fumarate and  $\mathrm{Me}_{2}\mathrm{SO}$   respiration, NADH dehydrogenase I has to use the nap h tho quin ones (men aquino ne and de meth lyme na quin one) as the redox mediators (Wissenbach et al.,  1 990;  1 992; Tran et al.,  1 997). The redox reaction NADH  $\longrightarrow$  quinone has to provide the energy for the translocation of the protons over the membrane. With the electro-negative menaquinone, the translocation of maximally    $1.7\;\mathrm{H^{+}/e^{-}}$    can be driven at the    $\varDelta p$   values reported here (Table 5). Therefore, it was argued that high  $\varDelta p$   values would not be compatible with the functioning of NADH dehydrogenase I in fumarate or  $\mathrm{Me}_{2}\mathrm{SO}$   respiration as a  $\mathrm{H^{+}}$    pump (Bongaerts et al.,  1 995). However, with the more electro positive (minor) de methyl men aquino ne which is also present under these conditions, maximal  $\mathrm{H^{+}/e^{-}}$    ratios of 2.5 can be calculated, enabling the function of NADH dehydrogenase I as a proton pump. Additionally, the   $\mathrm{NADH/NAD^{+}}$    ratios are supposed to be increased under anoxic growth conditions. By this, the actual redox potential of the NADH/NAD 1   couple and the redox difference between donor (NADH) and the acceptor (nap h tho quin one) would further increase, facilitating proton translocation. Thus, functioning of NADH dehydrogenase I as a proton pump is possible at the    $\varDelta p$   values determined here, but not at values of    $-100~\mathrm{mV}$   determined earlier.  

Significance of    $\mathbfcal{A}\,\pmb{\Psi}$   values of aerobic and anaerobic respiration for the regulation of facultative metabolism.  Changes in  $\varDelta\varPsi$   and/or  $\varDelta p$   have been suggested to provide the signal for the aerobic/anaerobic transition to membrane o us  $\mathrm{O}_{2}$  -sensors such as ArcB of  $E$  . coli  (Bogachev et al.,  1 993,  1 995). The small differences for    $\varDelta\varPsi$   and    $\varDelta p$   for aerobic and anaerobic respiration described here argue against such a signalling function. The same applies to    $\varDelta\mathfrak{p}\mathrm{H}$   which is negligible and does not change significantly; only for the switch to fermentation do larger differences exist. It is, therefore, doubtful whether    $\varDelta\,\mathrm{pH}$   or    $\varDelta\varPsi$   can signal the state of   $\mathrm{O}_{2}$   supply to  $\mathrm{O}_{2}$  -sensors such as ArcB.
